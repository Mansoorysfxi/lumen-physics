Why does water take so long to boil? Why does metal feel colder than wood at the same temperature? How does the Sun warm us across empty space? This unit answers all three, and you'll learn it by playing with heat.

What this unit is about

We will start with specific heat capacity, which is the heat needed to raise a substance's temperature. Water, with its high specific heat capacity, heats slowly but retains warmth longer, linking kitchen mysteries to broader climate issues. Next, we will look at the three main heat transfer methods: conduction, convection and radiation. Understanding these is crucial for grasping their roles in greenhouse effects and global warming. Finally, we'll explore geothermal phenomena, where the Earth's hot core drives magma movements and tectonic shifts.
